# Module 16 · Competition playbook

Winning teams tie a clear diagnosis of the client to a disciplined, well-documented strategy and defend it crisply. Wharton states that success "is not determined by portfolio performance"; teams are judged on the quality of the strategy, its fit with the client's objectives, the strength of the research and the communication ([Wharton Global Youth](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/investment-competition/about)).

## 16.1 How the 2026–27 competition works

All facts below come from Wharton's competition pages ([overview](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/competitions/investment-competition/), [rules and roles](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=16670), [FAQ](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/competitions/investment-competition/faq/)). The detailed instructions released to registered teams on September 15 govern formats and length limits, so check them for anything not covered here.

- **Teams:** four to six students from the same school, with a teacher advisor who may guide but may not make decisions or trade. The team leader must be at least 16.
- **Trading:** teams manage a portfolio in the Wharton Investment Simulator (WInS) from September 28 to December 4, 2026, buying and selling approved securities.
- **The case:** each year's client is a real Wharton alum with a financial scenario written for the competition.
- **Advancement:** the top 50 teams become semifinalists based on the strength of their IPS and final report. The top 10 compete at the Global Finale at Wharton on April 29–30.
- **Leaderboard:** the FAQ says WInS standings "have little to do with the final outcome."

| Deliverable | Due (5:00 p.m. ET) |
| --- | --- |
| Official team roster | Oct 9, 2026 |
| Trading Notes Analysis | Oct 23, 2026 |
| Investment Policy Statement | Nov 6, 2026 |
| Final report and school documentation | Dec 4, 2026 |

## 16.2 Rules that can disqualify you

- **Generative AI** may be used for brainstorming and idea generation, but AI-generated work may not be submitted as your own, and any AI-generated material you use must be cited. This course was drafted with an AI assistant: use it to learn, write every deliverable in your own words, and cite anything you take from it.
- **Academic integrity:** teams must follow Penn's Code of Academic Integrity. Plagiarism, fabricated analysis and misrepresenting decisions lead to disqualification.
- **Professional standards:** teams must follow the CFA Institute's Asset Manager Code.
- **No paid help:** paid advisors, education consultants and courses that teach the competition are prohibited.

## 16.3 The case-to-portfolio workflow

1. **Read the case three times.** First for the story, second to extract facts into the client profile ([Module 10](10-know-your-client.md)), third to collect quotes.
2. **Diagnose the client:** goals, horizon, required return, willingness, ability and need; agree an overall risk profile as a team.
3. **Form the market view:** a five-line macro outlook and a scenario table ([Module 2](02-economics-for-investors.md)), plus a cited set of capital market assumptions ([Module 12](12-portfolio-theory-and-asset-allocation.md)).
4. **Draft the IPS:** objectives, constraints, allocation ranges, benchmark and guidelines ([Module 11](11-investment-policy-statement.md)).
5. **Build and test the allocation:** compare two or three candidates on expected return, stress losses and probability of success (Modules 12 and 14).
6. **Select securities:** ETFs for each sleeve, plus individual stocks only where research earns them a place (Modules 3–8, 13).
7. **Implement and log:** trade with limit orders and record every reason in the trade log.
8. **Monitor weekly:** drift, risk dashboard, theses and news; rebalance by the IPS rules.
9. **Write the final report:** every claim traced to the client, the data or the IPS.
10. **Prepare to defend it:** presentation and Q&A drills if you reach the semifinals.

## 16.4 Blueprint for a conservative client

Ranges are a starting point; set the final weights from the client's own numbers.

| Sleeve | Typical range | Purpose | Tickers to research |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Liquidity reserve (T-bills, money market) | 5–15% | Near-term needs; ready cash | SGOV, BIL |
| Short-term Treasuries and short TIPS | 15–30% | Stability plus inflation protection | VGSH, VTIP, STIP |
| Core investment-grade bonds | 20–35% | Income; ballast in recessions | BND, AGG |
| Defensive and quality US stocks | 15–25% | Growth with smaller drawdowns | VIG, DGRO, USMV, QUAL |
| Broad US stocks | 5–15% | Market growth at low cost | VTI, VOO |
| International stocks | 5–10% | Diversification | VEA, IEFA |
| Real assets (gold, REITs) | 0–7% | Inflation and crisis hedge | GLDM, IAU, VNQ |
| Individual stocks (satellites) | 0–15% of the equity sleeve | Research conviction, growing dividends | Wide-moat dividend growers |

**Principles:** fund near-term needs first; keep bond duration short to intermediate; include explicit inflation protection; size stocks to the maximum loss the client accepts; test both a 2008-style crash and a 2022-style inflation shock; prefer simple, liquid, low-cost holdings; document everything.

**Traps:** an all-cash portfolio that fails the goal; long-duration bonds that fail in an inflation shock; "income" traps such as junk bonds or high-yield stocks with weak balance sheets; and hidden concentration, since the S&P 500's ten largest companies now make up more than a third of the index (approximate).

## 16.5 The final report

Follow the official format and length limits. A structure judges can navigate:

1. **Executive summary (one page):** the client in two sentences, the objectives, the allocation, the main risks and why this portfolio fits her.
2. **Client profile:** facts, goals, quotes, risk assessment.
3. **Investment Policy Statement:** a summary or the full IPS.
4. **Market outlook:** macro view, scenarios, capital market assumptions with sources.
5. **Strategy and asset allocation:** targets, ranges and why each sleeve exists.
6. **Security selection:** the role of each fund and a short thesis for each stock.
7. **Risk management:** risk dashboard, stress tests in dollars, Monte Carlo results, drawdown plan.
8. **Performance and monitoring:** results against the policy benchmark, attribution, lessons, the monitoring and rebalancing plan.
9. **Conclusion:** how the portfolio meets each goal.
10. **Appendix:** tear sheets, trade log, calculations, data sources and citations, including any AI-generated material.

**Writing rules:** open each section with its conclusion. Tie each decision to a client fact or quote. Use exhibits instead of paragraphs where possible: an allocation table, a stress-test table, the risk dashboard, a valuation football field, a Monte Carlo range. Make sure every number agrees across sections. Explain any jargon in plain English the first time it appears.

## 16.6 The presentation

| Slide | Headline (a full-sentence takeaway) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Our recommendation for the client in one sentence |
| 2 | Who she is and what she needs |
| 3 | Her objectives and constraints (the IPS) |
| 4 | The market outlook and what it means for her |
| 5 | The asset allocation and why each sleeve exists |
| 6 | Key holdings and their roles |
| 7 | Stress tests: what she could lose, in dollars |
| 8 | Performance so far and what we learned |
| 9 | How we will monitor and rebalance |
| 10 | Why this portfolio fits her |

Rehearse to time, give every member a speaking part, and end each answer by tying it back to the client.

## 16.7 Judge Q&A bank

| Likely question | What a strong answer covers |
| --- | --- |
| Why not put her entirely in Treasuries and CDs? | Required return vs the safe yield; inflation and shortfall risk; the probability-of-success comparison |
| How did you determine her risk tolerance? | Willingness, ability and need, each with evidence and quotes; why the more conservative view won |
| What is your expected return, and where does it come from? | The cited capital market assumptions and the weighted calculation; the range, not just a point |
| What happens if inflation returns to 5%? | Stress-test numbers; the roles of short duration, TIPS and real assets |
| What happens in a 2008-style crash? | Dollar loss against the IPS limit; the liquidity reserve; the rebalancing plan |
| Why this benchmark? | It mirrors the policy weights and meets the SAMURAI criteria |
| Why individual stocks instead of only ETFs? | The research edge, dividend reliability and diversification limits; or why you chose ETFs only |
| Why this particular fund? | Cost, tracking, liquidity, duration or holdings, and its role in one sentence |
| What would make you sell this stock? | The written sell criteria from the thesis |
| What is the biggest risk to your strategy? | Name it honestly, then give the mitigation and the warning signs you watch |
| How did you handle taxes? | Asset location, holding periods, loss harvesting, TIPS phantom income |
| How liquid is the portfolio if she needs cash tomorrow? | The share sellable within a day; the reserve sized to her known needs |
| Why international stocks? | Diversification, valuation, currency effects, size of the sleeve |
| Why gold, or why no alternatives? | Its role, its size and its stress-test behavior; or the reasons for exclusion |
| Your simulator return lagged. Why? | Attribution: allocation vs selection; the process held; what you would change |
| How does ESG factor in? | The client's stated values, or financial materiality; the effect on the benchmark |
| How would the portfolio change if she were 10 years older? | Horizon, human capital and withdrawal needs, leading to a lower equity share and more income |
| What if she wants to retire two years early? | Re-run the required return; the levers (save more, spend less, accept more risk) |
| How often will you rebalance, and why? | The calendar and threshold rule and its costs |
| What did you learn? | One specific insight about markets and one about the client |

## 16.8 Team process

| Role | Owns | Course modules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Team leader and portfolio manager | Integration, deadlines, consistency, final calls | 10–12, 16 |
| Client and IPS lead | Client profile, required return, IPS drafting | 10–11 |
| Macro and allocation analyst | Outlook, capital market assumptions, allocation, scenarios | 2, 12 |
| Equity analysts | Stock research, valuation, theses | 3–6, 13 |
| Fixed income and ETF analyst | Bonds, TIPS and fund selection | 7–9 |
| Risk and performance analyst | Stress tests, Monte Carlo, dashboard, attribution | 14–15 |

With four to six members, some people will hold two roles. Keep a weekly rhythm (a Monday portfolio check, midweek research, a Friday decision meeting), a shared decision log and one master spreadsheet so every number in the report comes from the same place.

## 16.10 Mistakes that cost points

- A strategy that would suit anyone, not this client.
- Chasing the leaderboard with risky trades that contradict the IPS.
- Inflation, taxes or fees ignored.
- Numbers that disagree between sections.
- Jargon with no plain-English explanation.
- Uncited data, or AI-generated material that is neither rewritten nor cited.
- Missing a deadline: each deliverable is due at 5:00 p.m. ET.

## 16.11 Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] Every goal in the case appears in the IPS with an amount and a date.
- [ ] Return and risk objectives are measurable and consistent with each other.
- [ ] The allocation sits inside the IPS ranges, and the benchmark matches it.
- [ ] Stress tests cover both a crash and an inflation shock, in dollars.
- [ ] Every holding has a stated role, and every stock has a thesis with sell criteria.
- [ ] The trade log explains every trade.
- [ ] Numbers agree across all sections and exhibits.
- [ ] Every source is cited, including any AI-generated material.
- [ ] A teammate who did not write it has proofread it.
- [ ] The official format and length rules are met, and the file is submitted before 5:00 p.m. ET.
