# Module 11 · Building the Investment Policy Statement

The Investment Policy Statement (IPS) is the contract between adviser and client: what the money is for, how much risk is acceptable, and the rules for managing it. In this competition the IPS is a graded deliverable of its own, due Nov 6, 2026, and every later decision in your final report should point back to it.

## 11.1 What an IPS does

- Defines the client's objectives and constraints in measurable terms.
- Sets the strategic asset allocation and the rules for managing it.
- Creates discipline: in a panic, the IPS decides what happens, not emotion.
- Defines how success will be measured and when the plan will be reviewed.
- Documents the agreed approach, which protects both client and adviser. Competition teams must follow the CFA Institute's Asset Manager Code, so write it as a fiduciary would.

## 11.2 The standard sections

1. **Scope and purpose:** who the client is, which assets the IPS covers and why it exists.
2. **Roles and responsibilities:** client, adviser and custodian, plus how the IPS is reviewed and amended.
3. **Investment objectives:** the return objective and the risk objective.
4. **Constraints:** time horizon, taxes, liquidity, legal and regulatory factors, unique circumstances.
5. **Strategic asset allocation:** target weights, permitted ranges and benchmarks.
6. **Investment guidelines:** permitted and prohibited investments, diversification limits, quality minimums.
7. **Rebalancing policy.**
8. **Monitoring, reporting and review.**
9. **Approval:** signatures and date.

## 11.3 Objectives and constraints: RRTTLLU

| Element | Question it answers | What to write | Example for a conservative client |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Return | What return does the client need, and want? | Required return (nominal and real), desired return, income needs | "A long-run average total return of at least 4.5% a year, about 2% after inflation, with priority on preserving capital" |
| Risk | How much risk can, and will, the client take? | Willingness + ability = overall tolerance, with numeric limits | "Below-average tolerance; a severe but plausible year should not cost more than 10% (\$50,000)" |
| Time horizon | When is the money needed? | Single or multi-stage, with dates | "Three stages: 3 years to a \$50,000 down payment, 10 years to retirement, then 25+ years of withdrawals" |
| Taxes | How will taxes affect returns? | Brackets, account types, unrealized gains | "24% federal and 5% state brackets; bonds and TIPS in the IRA, tax-efficient ETFs in the taxable account" |
| Liquidity | What cash is needed, and when? | Reserve, scheduled withdrawals, possible surprises | "\$50,000 in T-bills for the down payment; emergency fund held outside the portfolio" |
| Legal and regulatory | Are there legal limits? | Trusts, employer restrictions, prudent-investor standards | "No special legal constraints; managed to a fiduciary standard" |
| Unique circumstances | What else shapes the portfolio? | Values, concentrations, preferences | "Excludes tobacco and weapons manufacturers" |

## 11.4 Writing measurable objectives

| Vague (loses points) | Measurable (wins points) |
| --- | --- |
| "Grow her wealth safely" | "Earn at least 4.5% a year on average over rolling 5-year periods, after fees, while keeping any calendar-year loss under 10% in severe-but-plausible stress tests" |
| "Low risk" | "Target annual volatility of 5–7%, with a 95% one-year value at risk below 8%" |
| "Protect against inflation" | "Hold at least 25% in inflation-protected or short-duration assets, and beat CPI by 2 points a year over 10 years" |
| "Keep some cash" | "Hold 10% in T-bills to fund the \$50,000 down payment due in 3 years" |
| "Diversified" | "No single stock above 5%, no sector above 25% of the equity sleeve, at least 8 holdings" |

## 11.5 Strategic allocation, ranges and the policy benchmark

- **Targets and ranges:** give every asset class a target and a band, for example stocks 33% (28–38%), bonds 52% (47–57%), cash 10% (5–15%) and gold 5% (3–7%).
- **Benchmarks:** name an index for each asset class, then blend them at the target weights into a policy benchmark, for example 33% MSCI ACWI + 52% Bloomberg US Aggregate + 10% 3-month T-bill index + 5% gold.
- **What makes a good benchmark (SAMURAI):** Specified in advance, Appropriate, Measurable, Unambiguous, Reflective of current investment opinions, Accountable (the manager accepts it) and Investable.
- **Two lenses:** a relative target (beat the policy benchmark after fees) and an absolute target (for example CPI + 2%, or the required return).

## 11.6 Investment guidelines

- **Permitted:** for example, US-listed stocks, ETFs, mutual funds and Treasuries. Adjust to what the simulator allows.
- **Prohibited:** margin, short selling, leveraged and inverse ETFs, crypto, private placements and any excluded industries.
- **Diversification limits:** for example, no single stock above 5%, no sector above 25% of the equity sleeve, and no single non-index fund above 30%.
- **Quality:** investment-grade bonds only (average A or better) within a stated duration band, such as 2–5 years.
- **Liquidity:** every holding tradable daily.
- **Values:** any ESG or exclusion screens the client requested.

## 11.7 Rebalancing, monitoring and review

- **Rebalancing:** check monthly or quarterly. Rebalance when an asset class leaves its range, and at least once a year. Use new cash flows first to reduce trading and taxes.
- **Monitoring:** performance against the policy benchmark and the absolute target, risk metrics against limits, each holding against its thesis and sell criteria, and the client's circumstances.
- **Reporting:** quarterly reports and an annual review meeting.
- **Review triggers:** a change in goals, income, health, family or tax law, or a major market event. Review the IPS at least once a year regardless.

## 11.8 IPS template

Copy this skeleton and fill every bracket from your client profile ([Module 10](10-know-your-client.md)).

```markdown
# Investment Policy Statement: [Client name]
Prepared by [team name] on [date]. Next review: [date + 12 months].

## 1. Scope and purpose
[Who the client is, which assets this IPS covers (amount, accounts) and its purpose.]

## 2. Roles and responsibilities
Client: [provides information, approves the IPS, reports changes in circumstances]
Adviser: [manages the portfolio under this IPS as a fiduciary, reports quarterly]
Custodian: [holds assets, executes trades]

## 3. Return objective
Required return: [x.x% nominal, x.x% real] to fund [goals].
Target: [x.x% average annual total return after fees over rolling 5-year periods].

## 4. Risk objective
Willingness: [level, with evidence]. Ability: [level, with evidence]. Overall: [level].
Limits: [maximum calendar-year loss in stress tests, x% ($x); volatility target x–y%; 95% VaR].

## 5. Constraints
Time horizon: [stages and dates]
Liquidity: [amounts and dates; reserve]
Taxes: [brackets; which assets go in which account]
Legal and regulatory: [none, or details]
Unique circumstances: [values, preferences, concentrations]

## 6. Strategic asset allocation
| Asset class | Target | Range | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| [asset class] | [x%] | [x–y%] | [index] |
Policy benchmark: [weights × indexes]

## 7. Investment guidelines
Permitted: [...]. Prohibited: [...].
Limits: [single security, sector, credit quality, duration].

## 8. Rebalancing policy
[Ranges, calendar rule, use of cash flows]

## 9. Monitoring and review
[Reports, metrics, review triggers, annual review]

## 10. Approval
Client: __________  Adviser: __________  Date: __________
```

## 11.9 Mistakes judges catch

- Objectives that cannot be measured.
- A return objective that contradicts the risk objective, such as 8% a year with a 5% maximum loss.
- Ignoring inflation, taxes or fees.
- No reserve for known withdrawals.
- An allocation that contradicts the IPS, such as a "conservative" client with 70% in stocks.
- No benchmark, or a benchmark that does not match the allocation.
- No rebalancing rule.
- Generic text that could describe any client.
- Case facts copied wrongly, or arithmetic errors.

## Apply it to your case

- Draft Laura's IPS from the template, filling every field from your client profile.
- Check consistency: the return target must not exceed what the allocation can reasonably earn, and the stress-test losses must stay inside the risk limits.
- Ask a teammate who did not write it to try to break it: a vague phrase, a missing constraint or a number that does not tie out.
