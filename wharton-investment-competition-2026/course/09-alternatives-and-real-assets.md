# Module 9 · Alternatives and real assets

Alternatives can diversify a portfolio and hedge inflation, but they bring complexity, higher fees and often illiquidity. For a conservative client, use them sparingly and only through liquid, transparent vehicles you can explain in one sentence.

## 9.1 Why consider alternatives

- **Diversification:** returns that do not move with stocks and bonds, especially in the crises when both fall together.
- **Inflation protection:** real assets (property, commodities, infrastructure) tend to hold value when prices rise.
- **Income:** rents, royalties and private lending.
- **The costs:** fees, lock-ups, opaque pricing, complex taxes and manager risk. Reported returns of private assets are smoothed, which makes them look less volatile than they really are.

## 9.2 Real estate and REITs

- **Equity REITs** own property (apartments, warehouses, data centers, cell towers, offices, shopping centers, healthcare, self-storage). **Mortgage REITs** own property loans and are far more rate-sensitive and leveraged.
- **Rules:** a REIT must distribute at least 90% of its taxable income, which is why yields are high.
- **Metrics:** funds from operations (FFO) and adjusted FFO (AFFO) instead of EPS, net asset value (NAV), occupancy, same-store net operating income (NOI) growth and leverage.
- **Cap rate** = NOI ÷ property value. Rising interest rates push cap rates up and property values down.
- **Tax:** REIT dividends are mostly taxed as ordinary income, so they belong in tax-advantaged accounts where possible.
- **Behavior:** listed REITs act like stocks in the short run (they fell sharply in 2008 and 2020) and like property over the long run.

## 9.3 Commodities

- **Sectors:** energy, industrial metals, precious metals, agriculture and livestock.
- **Where the return comes from:** the change in spot prices, plus roll yield, plus interest on the cash collateral backing the futures.
- **Contango vs backwardation:** in contango, later futures cost more than near ones, so rolling them loses money over time; in backwardation, rolling earns money.
- **Role:** commodities are among the few assets that tend to rise with unexpected inflation, often because they cause it. But they pay no income, swing wildly and have had decade-long droughts.
- **Vehicles:** broad commodity ETFs such as PDBC (structured to avoid K-1 tax forms) or the equities of commodity producers.

## 9.4 Gold

From 1971, when gold began trading freely, to 2023, gold compounded at 7.87% a year, but with 26.4% annual volatility, a worst year of −32.6% (1981) and losses in 19 of 53 years. Its correlation with US stocks over that period was −0.20 ([Damodaran Online](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histret.html)).

- **Drivers:** real interest rates (gold tends to rise when real yields fall), the dollar, central-bank buying, and fear in crises.
- **Role:** a crisis and debasement hedge that pays no income. Allocations of 0–10% are common, and around 5% is easier to defend for a conservative client.
- **Vehicles:** physically backed ETFs such as GLDM, IAU or GLD. In the US, gains on these are taxed at the collectibles rate, up to 28% for long-term gains.

## 9.5 Private equity, venture capital, private credit and infrastructure

- **Structure:** limited partnerships with 7–12 year lives, capital called over time, and fees often around "2 and 20" (a 2% management fee and 20% of profits).
- **The J-curve:** early returns are negative because of fees and young investments; gains come later.
- **Measuring performance:** IRR, multiple on invested capital (MOIC), total value to paid-in (TVPI) and distributions to paid-in (DPI). DPI is the cash actually returned.
- **Access:** most funds require accredited investors (roughly \$1 million of net worth excluding the home, or about \$200,000 of annual income, \$300,000 jointly) or qualified purchasers (\$5 million of investments).
- **Fit:** rarely suitable for a conservative client, and not tradable in a simulator. Listed proxies exist (business development companies, listed asset managers, infrastructure ETFs), but they behave much like stocks.

## 9.6 Hedge funds and liquid alternatives

| Strategy | What it does | Behavior in sell-offs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long/short equity | Buys favored stocks and shorts disliked ones | Falls less than the market, but still falls |
| Market neutral | Balances longs and shorts to remove market exposure | Low correlation with stocks |
| Global macro | Bets on rates, currencies and commodities using economic views | Varies by manager |
| Managed futures (trend following) | Follows price trends across many futures markets | Often helps in long sell-offs; did well in 2022 as stocks and bonds fell together |
| Merger arbitrage | Buys takeover targets and captures the deal spread | Steady, with occasional losses when deals break |
| Event-driven and credit | Bets on restructurings and distressed debt | Hurt in credit crises |

Liquid alternative ETFs and mutual funds bring these strategies in a daily-traded wrapper at lower cost. Tickers to research: DBMF and KMLM (managed futures), MNA (merger arbitrage). They are diversifiers at a 0–5% weight, not core holdings.

## 9.7 Digital assets

- **What they are:** cryptocurrencies such as bitcoin and ether, with no cash flows and returns from price changes only.
- **Access:** the SEC approved spot bitcoin ETFs in January 2024.
- **Risks:** extreme volatility (bitcoin has had several drawdowns of more than 70%), regulatory change, custody and fraud.
- **Fit:** generally unsuitable for a risk-averse client. If a client insists, cap it at a token amount they can afford to lose entirely, and document the request.

## 9.8 Private-banking products: annuities and structured notes

Private banks often offer these to conservative clients, so expect judges to ask about them.

- **Annuities:** a single-premium immediate annuity (SPIA) turns a lump sum into guaranteed lifetime income and insures against outliving your money (longevity risk). Multi-year guaranteed annuities (MYGAs) work like insurance-company CDs. Fixed indexed annuities credit part of an index's gain with a 0% floor, subject to caps. Variable annuities invest in funds and can carry total fees of 2–3% a year. Watch for surrender charges, and remember that guarantees depend on the insurer's strength.
- **Structured notes:** bank-issued notes that combine a bond with options, such as principal protection with capped upside, or a buffer against the first 10–20% of losses. Downsides: the issuing bank's credit risk (some Lehman Brothers "principal-protected" notes lost most of their value in 2008), embedded fees, poor liquidity and complexity.
- **The test:** could you explain the product's payoff, costs and worst case to the client in plain English? If not, it does not belong in a conservative portfolio.

## 9.9 Alternatives at a glance

| Alternative | Liquidity | Cost | Correlation with stocks | Inflation hedge | Fit for a conservative client |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listed REITs | Daily | Low | Moderate to high | Partial | Small allocation for income |
| Broad commodities | Daily | Low to moderate | Low | Good against surprises | Small, optional |
| Gold ETF | Daily | Low | Low to negative | Partial | 0–5% as a crisis hedge |
| Managed futures fund | Daily | Moderate | Low | Varies | Small diversifier |
| Private equity and credit | Locked up for years | High | Moderate (understated) | Partial | Rarely |
| Hedge fund (direct) | Monthly or quarterly at best | High | Low to moderate | Varies | Rarely |
| Bitcoin ETF | Daily | Low to moderate | Unstable | Unproven | Generally no |
| Annuities | Poor (surrender charges) | Low to high | Not applicable | Only with an inflation rider | Possible for lifetime income |
| Structured notes | Poor | Embedded | Depends on the design | No | Only if fully understood |

## Apply it to your case

- Decide whether any alternative earns a place by solving a specific problem in Laura's profile, such as inflation protection or crisis diversification.
- If you include one, cap its weight, explain its role in one sentence and show its effect in your stress tests.
- If you exclude alternatives, say why in the report (complexity, illiquidity, cost). Judges reward a deliberate "no" as much as a justified "yes".
