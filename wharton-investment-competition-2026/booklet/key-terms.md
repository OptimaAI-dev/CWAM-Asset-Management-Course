## Key terms, part 2: concepts and jargon by theme

Plain-English definitions of the words that appear in research, client meetings and judges' questions.

### Markets and trading

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Bull market | A sustained rise in prices, often defined as 20% or more from a low |
| Bear market | A fall of 20% or more from a peak |
| Correction | A fall of 10–20% from a recent high |
| Rally, sell-off | A sharp rise; a sharp, broad fall |
| Volatility | How much prices swing, usually measured by standard deviation |
| Liquidity | How quickly an asset can be sold close to its fair value |
| Bid, ask, spread | The best price buyers pay, the best price sellers accept, and the gap between them |
| Market, limit and stop orders | Fill now at any price; fill only at your price or better; trigger a market order when a price is hit |
| Short selling | Selling borrowed shares in the hope of buying them back cheaper |
| Margin | Money borrowed from a broker to buy securities |
| Leverage | Using debt or derivatives to magnify exposure, and so gains and losses |
| Market capitalization | Share price × shares outstanding |
| Free float | Shares available for public trading |
| Index, benchmark | A basket that measures a market; the yardstick a portfolio is judged against |
| Blue chip | A large, established, financially sound company |
| Ex-dividend date | The first day a buyer no longer receives the upcoming dividend |
| Stock split | More shares at a proportionally lower price; total value unchanged |
| Buyback | A company repurchasing its own shares |
| Dilution | New shares shrinking existing owners' percentage stake |
| Capitulation | Panicked, high-volume selling, often near a market bottom |
| Dead-cat bounce | A brief rebound in a falling market |
| Circuit breaker | An automatic trading halt after a large market drop |
| Settlement | The transfer of cash and securities after a trade (T+1 in the US) |

### Investing styles and strategies

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Value investing | Buying assets priced below their estimated intrinsic value |
| Growth investing | Buying companies expected to grow faster than average |
| GARP | Growth at a reasonable price: a blend of growth and value |
| Quality investing | Favoring profitable, low-debt, stable companies |
| Momentum | Buying recent winners and avoiding recent losers |
| Income investing | Focusing on dividends and interest |
| Dividend growth investing | Owning companies with long records of raising dividends |
| Contrarian | Going against the crowd |
| Passive investing | Tracking an index at low cost |
| Active management | Trying to beat a benchmark through selection or timing |
| Factor (smart-beta) investing | Tilting toward characteristics linked to excess returns or lower risk |
| Core-satellite | A low-cost index core plus targeted active or thematic satellites |
| Buy and hold | Owning for the long run and trading rarely |
| Sector rotation | Shifting between sectors as the business cycle turns |
| Thematic investing | Concentrating on a trend such as clean energy or robotics |
| ESG and impact investing | Including environmental, social and governance factors; targeting measurable positive outcomes |
| Dollar-cost averaging | Investing equal amounts at regular intervals |
| Asset location | Choosing which account (taxable, deferred, Roth) holds each asset |
| Tax-loss harvesting | Realizing losses to offset taxable gains |

### Bonds and interest rates

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Par (face) value | The amount repaid at maturity, usually \$1,000 or \$100 |
| Coupon | The bond's fixed interest payment |
| Premium, discount bond | A bond priced above or below par |
| Callable, puttable bond | The issuer may repay early; the holder may demand early repayment |
| Convertible bond | A bond that can be exchanged for shares |
| Zero-coupon bond | Pays no coupons; bought at a discount and repaid at par |
| Covenant | A promise in a bond contract that protects lenders |
| Seniority | The order in which creditors are repaid in bankruptcy |
| Investment grade, high yield | Rated BBB−/Baa3 or better; rated below that ("junk") |
| Fallen angel | A bond downgraded from investment grade to high yield |
| Credit spread | Extra yield over a Treasury of the same maturity |
| Duration | A bond's sensitivity to interest rates, in years |
| Convexity | The curvature that makes price gains exceed losses for equal rate moves |
| Ladder, barbell, bullet | Maturities spread evenly; clustered at both ends; clustered around one date |
| Yield curve | Yields plotted against maturity |
| Inverted curve | Short-term yields above long-term yields; often a recession warning |
| Steepening, flattening | The gap between long and short yields widening or narrowing |
| Term premium | Extra yield demanded for lending longer |
| Real yield | Yield after inflation; TIPS quote it directly |
| Breakeven inflation | Nominal Treasury yield minus the TIPS yield of the same maturity |
| Flight to quality | Investors rushing into Treasuries and cash during turmoil |
| Reinvestment risk | The risk of reinvesting coupons or maturing bonds at lower rates |

### Economics

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Recession | In NBER's words, a significant decline in economic activity spread across the economy and lasting more than a few months |
| Soft landing, hard landing | Inflation cooled without a recession; cooled by causing one |
| Stagflation | High inflation with weak growth and rising unemployment |
| Deflation, disinflation, reflation | Falling prices; slowing inflation; inflation picking back up |
| Hawkish, dovish | Leaning toward higher rates to fight inflation; toward lower rates to support growth |
| Tightening, easing | Raising rates or shrinking the balance sheet; cutting rates or expanding it |
| Neutral rate (r\*) | The real rate that neither speeds up nor slows down the economy |
| Output gap | Actual output minus potential output |
| Goldilocks | Growth neither too hot nor too cold, with tame inflation |
| Core inflation | Inflation excluding volatile food and energy |
| Sticky inflation | Prices, often services and wages, that adjust slowly |
| Base effects | Year-over-year rates distorted by unusual readings a year earlier |
| Wage-price spiral | Rising wages pushing up prices, which push up wages |
| Fiscal stimulus, austerity | Higher spending or tax cuts; spending cuts or tax rises |
| Debt ceiling | The legal cap on US federal borrowing |
| Trade deficit | Imports exceeding exports |
| Tariff | A tax on imports |
| Reserve currency | A currency held widely by central banks, chiefly the US dollar |
| Safe haven | An asset expected to hold value in a crisis, such as Treasuries or gold |
| Productivity | Output per hour worked; the engine of long-run growth |

| Beta | Sensitivity to market moves |
| Tracking error | Volatility of returns relative to the benchmark |

| Home bias | Overweighting one's own country |
| Sequence-of-returns risk | Damage from poor returns early in a withdrawal period |
| Longevity risk | Outliving one's money |
| Shortfall risk | The chance of missing a goal |
| Tail risk, black swan, fat tails | Rare extreme losses; an unforeseeable shock; extremes more common than a bell curve predicts |
| Drawdown | A decline from a previous peak |
| Mean reversion | The tendency of values to drift back toward an average |
| Survivorship bias | Overstated results because failures dropped out of the data |

| Impairment | A write-down when an asset is worth less than its book value |
| Working capital | Current assets minus current liabilities |
| Operating leverage | High fixed costs that make profits swing more than sales |
| Guidance | Management's own forecast |
| Consensus estimate | The average analyst forecast |
| Beat, miss | Results above or below consensus |
| Non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings | Earnings that exclude items management considers unusual; read the reconciliation |
| Restatement | Correcting previously published financial statements; a red flag |
| Spin-off | Separating a division into an independent listed company |
| Moat | A durable competitive advantage |
| Synergy | Value from combining two companies beyond their separate values |
| Accretive, dilutive | A deal that raises or lowers earnings per share |
| Hurdle rate | The minimum return a project must earn |
| Economic profit | Profit above the cost of all capital: (ROIC − WACC) × invested capital |

### Behavioral finance

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Prospect theory | People judge outcomes as gains or losses from a reference point and weigh losses more |
| Loss aversion | Losses hurt roughly twice as much as equal gains feel good |
| Anchoring | Fixating on an initial number such as a purchase price |
| Confirmation bias | Seeking information that supports existing beliefs |
| Overconfidence | Overrating one's skill or knowledge |
| Recency bias | Overweighting recent events |
| Herding | Following the crowd |
| Disposition effect | Selling winners too soon and holding losers too long |
| Mental accounting | Treating money differently depending on its source or label |
| Framing | Decisions changing with how the same facts are presented |
| Status quo bias, regret aversion | Preferring inaction; avoiding decisions that might later be regretted |
| Hindsight bias | Believing past events were predictable |
| Availability bias | Judging likelihood by how easily examples come to mind |

### Wealth management, regulation and ethics

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Fiduciary duty | The legal duty to act in the client's best interest, putting the client first |
| Suitability | Recommendations must fit the client's profile |
| Regulation Best Interest | SEC rule requiring broker-dealers to act in retail clients' best interest when recommending |
| Know your customer | Verifying identity and understanding the client before advising |
| Discretionary vs advisory mandate | The manager trades without prior approval; or recommends and the client decides |
| Custodian | The institution that holds client assets |
| Trust (revocable, irrevocable) | A legal arrangement holding assets for beneficiaries; changeable or fixed |
| Estate planning | Arranging the transfer of wealth: will, trusts, beneficiaries, powers of attorney |
| Step-up in basis | Heirs' cost basis resets to market value at death under current US law |
| Family office | A firm managing a wealthy family's investments and affairs |
| Accredited investor | Meets SEC wealth or income tests to buy private offerings |
| Fee-only vs commission-based adviser | Paid only by the client; paid by product sales, which can create conflicts |
| Wrap fee | One fee covering advice, trading and custody |
| Robo-adviser | Automated, algorithm-driven portfolio management |
| Prudent investor rule | Standard requiring trustees to invest with care, skill and diversification |
| CFA Institute Code and Standards | Ethics rules for investment professionals: integrity, objectivity, loyalty to clients |
| Asset Manager Code | CFA Institute's code for asset managers, which competition teams must follow |
| Insider trading | Trading on material non-public information; illegal |
| Front-running | Trading ahead of a client's order for personal gain; prohibited |
| Churning | Excessive trading to generate commissions; prohibited |
| Conflict of interest | Any situation where the adviser's interests could compete with the client's; must be disclosed and managed |
